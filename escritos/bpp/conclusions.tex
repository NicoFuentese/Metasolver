\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We showed that a beam search for the single container loading problem becomes a strong basis for the
three-dimensional bin packing problem when it is guided by profits per box type instead of volumes: in a single
construction this change alone saves 2.8\% of the bins, and the parameters of the beam search need no retuning.
On this basis we presented a method that generates bin patterns with the
single-bin loader inside a squeaky wheel loop over the profits of the box types, enlarges the pool with
the loads that the loader explores and discards, and recombines the pool with a set covering model, or
with a set partitioning model when the bins must respect vertical support. The LP relaxation of the
pool indicates when the recombination has nothing more to offer, which happens in most runs, so that an
integer program is rarely solved.

On the benchmark of \citet{Martello2000} the method obtains totals at the level of the best published
ones without rotations: \cfgqual{} obtains 7249.3 bins on classes 1 and 4--8, against 7252 for
\citet{Mahvash2018}, and \cfgfast{}, with 6.7 s per instance, obtains 7256.7, against 7258 for BRKGA. With rotations
the three configurations are below the total of \citet{Mahvash2018} on the eight classes (9004.0, 8993.7 and
8982.3 against 9013), although the margin is small in classes 2 and 3. The squeaky wheel loop accounts for most of
the improvement over a single construction. The recombination saves a further 10 to 17 bins with respect to the loop alone
with the same time, also on instances that were not used to design the method, and the harvest makes it effective
with short budgets. On industrial instances with 70\% support on every box, the construction alone, with a time of 2 s per bin,
uses 6.0\% fewer bins than the column generation over layers of \citet{Elhedhli2019} when $n\ge 500$, and the squeaky wheel loop
uses 3.8\% fewer when $n<500$. In these instances the partitioning recombination did not improve any run, so the evidence for
the recombination comes from instances without support, and what improves the industrial results is the time spent in each bin.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The recombination can only choose among the patterns in the pool. With several hundred boxes the pools
of our budgets leave little room: on 36 instances with 500 and 1000 boxes the recombination improved the solution in 7 of 108 runs of \cfgfast{},
in 1 of \cfgbal{} and in none of \cfgqual{}. The quality then depends on the generation of patterns and not on the recombination.
The covering model requires that removing a box keeps a bin feasible; the partitioning model removes that requirement at the price of a
harder model, and we found no instance with support in which it pays. The parameters of the squeaky wheel loop
and of the harvest were chosen on the original instances. The comparisons with the literature are made on
totals, from other machines and with an unknown number of runs, and the advantage in time of \cfgqual{} over the column generation of
\citet{Mahvash2018} holds only from about 150 boxes on.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The generator receives no information from the recombination. Using the dual prices of the pool as
profits for BSG, as column generation does, did not pay with our budgets, but it could do so with longer ones.
Other loading constraints that a single-bin loader can enforce, such as weight limits, load balance or fragility,
could be handled with the same scheme and the partitioning model.
