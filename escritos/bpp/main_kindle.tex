\begin{abstract}
We show that a beam search for the single container loading problem is a strong basis for the
three-dimensional bin packing problem when its objective is changed from the loaded volume to a profit per
box type that favours large boxes. The bins are loaded one at a time, a squeaky wheel loop learns the profits,
and the loading constraints, such as vertical support, are enforced inside the beam search. On top of this
generator, the bins built during the search, together with complete loads that the beam search explores and
discards, are recombined with a set covering model, or with a set partitioning model when the boxes must have
vertical support. The LP relaxation of the pool proves in most runs that the incumbent is the best
combination of the pool, so that the integer program is rarely needed. On the benchmark of Martello,
Pisinger and Vigo the method obtains totals at the level of the best published ones with fixed
orientation, and below the best published column generation with rotations (8982.3 bins against 9013
on the eight classes). On industrial instances with 70\% support on every box, the sequential construction alone uses 6.0\%
fewer bins than a column generation over layers when the instances have 500 boxes or more; there the
recombination is applicable but does not improve any solution.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} three-dimensional bin packing; set covering; squeaky wheel optimisation;
beam search; vertical support.
